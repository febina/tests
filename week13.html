🌱 Learning Module A: Roots, Prefixes & Suffixes

🔍
Detective Root

"Every giant word has a secret heart! A root word (or base word) holds the word's primary meaning. When mischievous characters add pieces to the front or end, the word takes on a brand-new job!"

Prefix: Added to the front of a root word (e.g., un- + happy = unhappy - meaning not happy)[cite: 3, 8].

Suffix: Added to the end of a root word (e.g., help + -ful = helpful - meaning full of help)[cite: 3, 38].

Spelling Changes: Sometimes root words change before taking a suffix! Watch how happy drops the y and turns into an i to become happiness[cite: 3, 8]!

⚡ Learning Module B: The Four 'Shun' Sounds

🦸‍♂️
Captain Suffix

"Greetings Detective! Words that end with the sound /ʃən/ ('shun') sound identical when spoken aloud, but their spellings depend strictly on the root word's family: -tion, -sion, -ssion, or -cian! Never guess—track the clues!"[cite: 26, 27]

B1. Words Ending in -tion

The most common 'shun' ending! Used heavily with verbs ending in -t or -te[cite: 28, 29].

Ends in -t:
invent → invention
prevent → prevention
protect → protection
correct → correction
inspect → inspection[cite: 29]
Ends in -te (drop the e):
create → creation
educate → education
operate → operation
communicate → communication
celebrate → celebration[cite: 28, 29]

B2. Words Ending in -ssion

Used when root words end in double-s (-ss) or -mit![cite: 28, 30]

Ends in -mit (changes to -ssion):
admit → admission
permit → permission
commit → commission
transmit → transmission
submit → submission[cite: 30]
Ends in -ss (keep both s's):
discuss → discussion
possess → possession
confess → confession
express → expression
impress → impression[cite: 30]

B3. Words Ending in -sion

Often used when the root word ends in -d, -de, or -se[cite: 28, 31].

Ends in -de / -d:
conclude → conclusion
include → inclusion
expand → expansion
extend → extension
persuade → persuasion[cite: 31]
Ends in -se:
revise → revision
supervise → supervision
confuse → confusion
diffuse → diffusion[cite: 31]

B4. Words Ending in -cian

Almost always describes a person with a specific trade, talent, or skill! Frequently relates to roots ending in -c or -cs[cite: 32].

Base Subject / Root Person (-cian) Meaning / What this person does
music musician A person who plays, composes, or performs music[cite: 32].
magic magician A skilled entertainer who performs illusions and tricks[cite: 32].
clinic clinician A health professional who works directly with patients in a clinic[cite: 32].
electric electrician A skilled tradesperson who installs and repairs electrical wiring[cite: 32].
politics politician A person involved in government and public policy[cite: 32].
mathematics mathematician An expert who studies numbers, patterns, and mathematical proofs[cite: 32].

⚖️ Learning Module C: The -ance vs -ence Balance Lab

👽
The Exception Alien

"Greetings Earthling! Many students find -ance and -ence confusing because both endings sound identical! But if you look at their base families and the sound of letters 'c' and 'g', the clues will reveal themselves!"[cite: 12, 17, 22, 27]

1. The Golden Clues for -ance

  • Base adjectives ending in -ant: Replace -ant with -ance (significant → significance, abundant → abundance, brilliant → brilliance, arrogant → arrogance, elegant → elegance)[cite: 14].
  • Verbs ending in -ure or -ear: (endure → endurance, appear → appearance, assure → assurance, clear → clearance)[cite: 15].
  • Verbs ending in -y: Change y to i and add -ance (defy → defiance, ally → alliance, rely → reliance)[cite: 16].
  • Hard 'c' or 'g' sounds: If the base letter has a hard sound like c in cat or g in game (e.g. elegance, significance), it takes -ance![cite: 17]

2. The Golden Clues for -ence

  • Base adjectives ending in -ent: Replace -ent with -ence (confident → confidence, innocent → innocence, patient → patience, absent → absence, obedient → obedience)[cite: 21, 23].
  • Verbs ending in -ere: Drop the final e and add -ence (revere → reverence, adhere → adherence, interfere → interference)[cite: 20].
  • Soft 'c' or 'g' sounds: If the sound is soft like c in cell or g in gin (e.g. adolescence, emergence), it takes -ence![cite: 22]
👽 Alien Alert: Watch the Famous Exceptions!

• perseverance: Even though it comes from persevere, it is spelled with -ance![cite: 20]

• vengeance: Even though it has a soft 'g' sound, it keeps the letters -eance![cite: 22]

• tolerate vs educate: tolerate becomes tolerance (-ance), but educate becomes education (-tion)[cite: 18]. Always observe the word family!
